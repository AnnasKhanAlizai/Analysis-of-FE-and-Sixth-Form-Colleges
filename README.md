Statistical Analysis
To examine the effects of the different variables we performed different tests. Let us discuss them one by one.
Examining Regional effect: According to the one-way ANOVA test we examine CH per learner across the nine different regions. The output stats indicate that regions are associated with the significant effects on CH per learner(R square = 0.036188), which implies the CH among the regions are not uniform. We can see from box plot and the table  Northwest and Greater London has high mean values while Southwest and East midlands has the lowest showing variations among the region with respect to CH per learner. Most important thing is our variance is unequal across the region P=0.0012, violates the homogeneity of variance assumptions also the sample size is large and because of Central Limit Theorem ANOVA behaves robust.
    
Examining Institution Type: According to the T-Test we found significant difference in contact hours per learner among the types of colleges, the FE college has much less CH per learner as compared to the sixth form. Tabular stats and plots as I analyze using both parametric and non-parametric methods and their output supports my comment, graphical output is shown in figure. Moreover, our variance is unequal p <  0.0001, violates the homogeneity of variance assumptions also the sample size is large, t-test behaves robust. In short, output shows strong evidence that institutional type matters with CH per learner.

    
The p<0.0001 indicates that CH Per Learner has significant difference between FE and Sixth Form colleges, also we can see their rank scores gape which are approximately 383 and 862 respectively. Moreover, the non-parametric test Kruskal-Wallis also verifies our previous words. i.e. (chi-square = 538.4721, p< 0.0001). The box plot verifies the above statement that the FE and Sixth form colleges both the groups are statistically significantly different. A clear plot indicates that Sixth form colleges have higher median and more concentrated CH Per learner distribution. On the other hand FE college have lower as well as lower variable scores.
Examining by Size effect: With the help of PROC GLM we analyze size categories which shows the strong influence on CH per learner means effect is significant as p = 0.0001. As our Levene p value is <0.0001 implies unequal but we have sample size almost equal, overall it will not impact much on our result output. Moreover, as we can see in boxplot smaller institutions tend to deliver high number of CH per learner then larger ones. Variation increases as size decreases, also small institutions showing widespread and outliers in plot. 
  
Examining the year effect (One- Way ANOVA): As we can see the tabular and graphical figures indicate that year does not significantly influenced/impact on the CH per learner also boxplot shows we have almost same median value.
    
Examining the gender effect: As we can see the high F and low P value as well as the graph having upward and downward slope and narrow shaded area along both the sides of  graduate percentage of males (GPercentMale) and graduate percentage of females (GpercentFemale) line all these things tells us that CH per learner are statistically significant, precise  and consistent, as R square is 0.0718 indicates effect size is small that is 7.18%. our model estimates for every 1% increase in male learners increase in 35.616 CH per learner while in female case for every 1% increase in female learners decrease in 35.616 CH per learner as screenshots are attached below. In simple words if higher proportion of male learners implies to deliver slightly more CH per learner.
   
   
Examining the VAF effect: Results shows highly significant positive relation between VAF and CH per learner. That is for every one unit increase in VAF cause an increase of 1106.89 in CH per learner. The upward slope and narrow confidence interval indicates its precision estimate. Also, estimate VAF regression value is 1106.897 and P<0.0001 implies higher the VAF values, deliver the higher CH per learner. Till now in our all-previous analysis VAF has got the second highest effects/impact on CH per learner after the Size.
   
Examining the Graduation passing rate effect: High value of F shows strong linear relationship and P values and graphical results indicates significant positive relation between Graduate passing rate and Contact Hour Per Learner. The upward graph trend and narrow confidence interval shows high precision estimate for the Graduation Passing rate (GradPR). Till now Graduation Passing rate is the third most important attribute which impact on CH per learner after Size and VAF.
